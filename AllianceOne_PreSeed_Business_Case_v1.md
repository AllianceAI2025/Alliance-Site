# AllianceOne Pre-Seed Business Case

**Prepared:** August 14, 2026  
**Financing objective:** $500,000 minimum close; approximately $530,000 target to preserve the full operating reserve  
**Stage:** Working product and synthetic-data demonstration; recruiting initial design partners; production writeback and enterprise readiness in progress  
**Purpose:** Investor case and operating plan. This is not legal, tax, accounting, or investment advice.

## 1. Executive case

Professional-services firms do not lack data. They lack a durable record of what an engagement was intended to accomplish, why it was structured a certain way, what was committed, how that commitment changed, and what actually happened.

CRM systems own opportunity state. PSA and project-management systems own execution state. ERP systems own billing and financial actuals. Email, meetings, and document systems hold the conversations and artifacts that explain the work. None of these systems owns the engagement's intent across its full lifecycle.

AllianceOne fills that gap.

The platform captures the first client signals, builds the pursuit narrative, supports scoping and proposal development, converts a won engagement into an approved delivery plan, materializes that plan into the firm's operating stack, gives delivery teams the context behind their work, reconciles committed versus delivered outcomes, and preserves the resulting precedent for the next engagement.

The investment case is not that AllianceOne replaces CRM, PSA, ERP, email, or document systems. It makes those systems behave as one engagement operating environment:

> AllianceOne owns intent. The operating stack owns execution.

The proposed $500,000 round is designed to prove one commercially complete loop for a narrow initial customer profile. It is not intended to finance broad market scale. The goal is to reach the next financing with paid customer evidence, repeatable implementation, measurable delivery economics, and enterprise security readiness.

## 2. Product and category definition

AllianceOne is an engagement-intent platform for project-based and professional-services firms. It connects the reasoning, commitments, approvals, and outcomes of an engagement across the systems that already run the firm.

### 2.1 The category

> AllianceOne is the system of record for engagement intent in professional services.

That does not mean AllianceOne owns every task, invoice, contact, or document. It owns the approved scope, plan, staffing model, effort assumptions, decision gates, changes, and the lineage connecting those commitments to delivery.

### 2.2 The engagement lifecycle

The product supports one continuous lifecycle:

1. Capture early signals before and after opportunity qualification.
2. Build a grounded pursuit brief from conversations and firm evidence.
3. Scope the engagement in Chat using precedent, methods, staffing, economics, and risk.
4. Generate and approve the internal scope and client proposal.
5. Convert the won proposal into a tentative engagement plan.
6. Select and approve the team, workstreams, deliverables, effort, and roadmap.
7. Write the approved plan to the PSA or project-management system.
8. Support delivery through consultant workspaces, research, firm methods, and engagement context.
9. Reconcile execution and billing actuals against the commitment.
10. Capture changes, decisions, outcomes, and lessons as reusable precedent.

The initial commercial wedge is deliberately narrow, while the engagement record compounds into a broader operating system as more lifecycle stages and execution systems are connected.

### 2.3 The initial customer profile

The strongest early customers are project-based firms that have:

- 50 to 500 employees, with exceptions where workflow complexity supports the economics;
- repeated scoping, staffing, pricing, and delivery decisions;
- a CRM, PSA or project tool, ERP or billing system, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, and fragmented document repositories;
- partner or practice-leader bottlenecks in pursuit and engagement design;
- meaningful scope leakage, write-offs, change-order risk, staffing friction, or delivery variance;
- enough engagement volume and precedent to make learning valuable;
- an executive buyer who owns delivery economics and can sponsor cross-system adoption.

The most likely initial segments remain management consulting, specialist advisory, technology and transformation consulting, and other precedent-rich consulting firms. Accounting and tax are credible adjacent segments, but their recurring engagement patterns, regulatory requirements, and buyer workflows should be modeled separately rather than quietly added to the initial beachhead.

### 2.4 The ROI thesis

Time saved on proposals is useful but insufficient. AllianceOne should be measured against engagement economics:

- better scoping and estimate accuracy;
- less unpriced scope expansion;
- higher change-order capture;
- faster conversion from won work to an executable plan;
- faster and better-supported staffing decisions;
- lower committed-versus-delivered margin variance;
- earlier detection of delivery risk;
- more consistent use of firm methods and precedent;
- less dependency on individual partners to reconstruct why decisions were made.

### 2.5 Security is part of the product and sales motion

AllianceOne will process sensitive client, commercial, staffing, and delivery information. Security architecture, data isolation, auditability, retention, contractual controls, and SOC readiness are not back-office tasks. They determine which design partners can deploy the product and how quickly enterprise procurement can approve it.

## 3. Market-sizing methodology and validation

### 3.1 Census foundation and count validation

The 2022 Statistics of U.S. Businesses dataset provides the public foundation for the bottom-up market model. The Census Bureau identifies it as the latest detailed SUSB data and reports firms, establishments, employment, payroll, and receipts by industry and enterprise size.

The market ranges should remain provisional until the following are completed:

- reproduce every firm count from the source workbook;
- retain a calculation file that maps each included NAICS code and employee band;
- model the 50 to 499 employee serviceable segment and document any justified exceptions;
- separate the consulting beachhead from accounting, tax, engineering, and advisory adjacencies;
- document exclusions so the same firm is not implicitly represented in multiple narratives;
- validate the modeled ACV by firm size through buyer interviews.

The modeled $392 million beachhead, $820 million SAM, and $2.73 billion TAM are useful directional envelopes. They should not be presented as fully validated market facts until the firm counts and price assumptions are independently reproduced.

### 3.2 Serviceable market constraints

A serviceable market depends on more than industry and headcount. It must also reflect product readiness:

- supported CRM and PSA combinations;
- supported identity, document, email, and meeting environments;
- security requirements the company can satisfy;
- implementation time and customer data quality;
- sales cycle and buyer authority;
- ability to demonstrate value before a broad rollout.

The first twelve-month SAM should therefore be expressed as a named-account universe, not only a national revenue number. A practical first target is 100 to 200 firms that match the stack, buyer, pain, and design-partner profile.

### 3.3 The SOM needs a capacity model

The arithmetic of 125 customers at $80,000 average ACV equals $10 million ARR. What is missing is the operating path:

- annual new-logo production;
- sales-cycle length and conversion rate;
- implementation capacity per engineer or implementation lead;
- customer success coverage;
- gross retention and expansion;
- partner-led versus AllianceOne-led implementation;
- cash required to finance the growth curve.

Until those assumptions exist, $10 million ARR is a credible destination, not yet a complete operating forecast.

### 3.4 The competitive framing needs to reflect complementarity

PSA, CRM, ERP, project-management, enterprise-search, and proposal products are all relevant competitors for budget and attention. AllianceOne's sharper position is that it coordinates them without claiming to replace their operational roles.

The durable differentiation is the engagement state and its lineage:

> client signal to scope to approved plan to system writeback to delivery actuals to reconciled precedent

Foundation-model providers can improve reasoning and retrieval. They do not, by themselves, define the firm's engagement ontology, maintain the canonical commitment, govern approvals, integrate execution receipts, or reconcile actual delivery against that commitment.

- A credible software gross-margin path after implementation services are separated.
- SOC 2 controls operational, with the appropriate Type I or Type II path timed to actual buyer requirements.

## 8. Initial commercial model

### Buyer

The economic buyer is likely the COO, managing partner, head of delivery, or practice leader responsible for growth and engagement economics. IT and security are required approvers. Engagement leaders are the operational champions. Consultants become daily users after work is won.

### Initial offer

The offer should sell a complete outcome, not access to a collection of AI features:

> Turn one practice's live pursuits and engagements into an approved, executable, and measurable engagement record connected to the firm's existing systems.

### Design-partner structure

A discounted design-partner agreement should purchase specific consideration:

- paid activation and recurring platform commitment;
- access to the required source systems and users;
- agreed baseline metrics;
- structured feedback cadence;
- permission to publish results after approval;
- defined conversion to standard pricing;
- bounded implementation scope.

An annual design-partner range of $30,000 to $50,000 and an implementation range of $25,000 to $50,000 are plausible planning hypotheses. They are not validated prices. The round should fund enough buyer work to establish which portion is software, which portion is implementation, and which value metric best predicts willingness to pay.

The bottom-up model supports the following cautious narrative, subject to the validation work described above:

The model indicates an initial annual software opportunity of roughly $392 million, an adjacent serviceable market of roughly $820 million, and a broader U.S. professional-services expansion market of roughly $2.7 billion. These figures are modeled from Census firm counts and pricing assumptions, not analyst-reported AllianceOne revenue.

The most persuasive venture-scale proof remains simple:

- 125 customers at $80,000 average ACV produce $10 million ARR.
- 1,000 customers at $100,000 average ACV produce $100 million ARR.

## 10. Why now

The market evidence supports the timing:

- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects professional, scientific, and technical services to be the second-fastest-growing sector from 2024 to 2034, adding an estimated 812,500 jobs.
- McKinsey reported that 88% of surveyed organizations used AI in at least one business function in 2025, while nearly two-thirds had not begun scaling it across the enterprise.
- Thomson Reuters reported in 2026 that professional-services organization-wide AI use had almost doubled to 40%, but only 18% of respondents said their organizations tracked AI ROI.
- Deltek's reporting on the 2026 SPI benchmark says average billable utilization fell to 66.4% in 2025 and average revenue leakage reached 4.5%.

These data do not prove demand for AllianceOne. They show the environment in which the product can be valuable: firms are adopting AI, struggling to operationalize it, and facing measurable pressure on delivery economics.

## 11. Investor objections the business case must answer

### "This is too broad for a small team."

Answer with the wedge. The company is not productionizing every lifecycle module for every stack in this round. It is proving a complete intent-to-execution loop for one customer profile and one primary operating stack.

### "Why will a PSA or CRM vendor not build this?"

Those systems optimize the state they already own. AllianceOne's value comes from preserving the reasoning and commitment that span systems, then reconciling them without requiring one vendor to become the entire operating stack.

### "Why can a foundation-model provider not do this?"

A model can reason over supplied context. The product challenge is maintaining governed engagement state, approval lineage, firm-specific ontology, system writeback, execution receipts, and learning across engagements. Model capability improves the product but does not replace those controls and integrations.

### "Will services destroy the software margin?"

The first deployments will contain services. The round must prove that connectors, mappings, ontology patterns, and onboarding can be reused. Implementation revenue should be reported separately from ARR, and internal deployment effort should be tracked from the first customer.

### "Is $500,000 enough for enterprise software?"

It is enough only if the scope remains narrow, the founder leads sales and product, the engineer is senior and product-oriented, early customers pay, and the reserve is protected. It is not enough for a broad enterprise go-to-market organization or many bespoke integrations.

### "What is the team risk?"

A single founder and one international contractor create continuity, technical-leadership, and investor-perception risk. The mitigation is strong IP assignment, clear technical ownership, documented architecture, disciplined deployment, and a plan to recruit a founding or senior engineering leader once commercial evidence justifies it.
